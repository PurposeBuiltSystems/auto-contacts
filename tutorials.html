Auto Contacts — Tutorial

The details you need are already in your mail — every signature block anyone has ever sent you carries a title, a phone number and an organisation. This reads them and files them, so you stop scrolling back through six months of email for a mobile number.

There are two ways to use it, and they're for different moments.

Tutorial 1 — one person, right now

Worked example: a consulting engineer has just emailed you and you'll be dealing with them for the next year.

1. With their email open, click Add to Contacts in the Auto Contacts ribbon group.
You should see a contact built from their signature — name, email, and whatever else the signature offered: phone, job title, company.
2. Check it, correct anything wrong, and save.

That's the whole flow. Use it as a habit whenever someone new turns out to matter.

Tutorial 2 — the backlog sweep

For the first run, when years of contacts were never filed.

1. Click Contact Sweep on the ribbon.
2. Set Days back. Start with something modest — 90 days — rather than reaching for everything at once.
3. Click Scan my sent mail.
You should see a list of people you have written to who aren't in your contacts yet, with whatever details their signatures gave up.
4. Tick the ones you actually want. Select all is there for when the list is genuinely all keepers.
5. Click Add selected.
Don't just select all on the first run. Scanning sent mail is the right signal — you wrote to these people, so there was a relationship — but a year of it includes one-off vendors, a helpdesk ticket, and someone you emailed once by mistake. Five hundred junk contacts is worse than none, because it poisons every autocomplete you'll ever type into. Sweep a quarter at a time and be choosy.

Why it reads sent mail rather than your inbox

Anyone can email you. The people worth filing are the people you replied to — that's a relationship rather than an arrival. It's also why the list is short enough to review.

About the signature parsing

Signature blocks are free text, and people format them however they like. The parser is deterministic pattern-matching — no AI — which makes it predictable and roughly 85% accurate.

Expect a few oddities: a job title that swallowed a department name, a fax number filed as a mobile, a legal disclaimer treated as an organisation. Glance down the column before you commit. Everything is editable afterwards in Outlook, and nothing is added without you ticking it.

What it never does

It doesn'tBecause
Add anyone automaticallyNothing is created until you tick it and click Add selected.
